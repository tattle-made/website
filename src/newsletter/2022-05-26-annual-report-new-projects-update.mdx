## Presenting Uli

![Uli](https://mcusercontent.com/a9af83af1f247ecc04f50ad46/images/1eaed960-a94b-d97f-1aef-893bb9b1d15d.png)

Our year-long project on [addressing online gender based violence](https://tattle.co.in/products/ogbv/) with the [Centre for Internet and Society](https://cis-india.org/) is now in its last stretch.

The plugin, now called Uli, is open to a [closed beta](https://twitter.com/cis_india/status/1527597502425206785).

In the beta, we've implemented the archiving and slur replacement feature - slurs found in a list crowdsourced from activists and researchers, are blurred from Twitter feeds of the plugin users.

It also lets users archive instances of abuse directed at them.

The final version, which will also include a feature for machine learning driven OGBV detection, is due for release in a month.

As anyone working on hate speech detection will tell you, the task is incredibly challenging. Working in Indian languages adds another dimension of complexity.

But we're excited with how Uli has turned out. More importantly, we're immensely proud of the collaborative process through which it was designed and built.

We are grateful to the team at CIS, as well as the many activists who have volunteered their time and expertise, for being ideal partners for this project.

## Three is The Magic Number:

We started three new projects this year!

Funded by the [Grant for the Web](https://www.grantfortheweb.org/blog/announcing-our-newest-cohort-of-awardees), we’re running a behavioural study with [Monk Prayogshala](https://www.monkprayogshala.in/) on incentives for sharing accurate information.

Monk Prayogshala is leading on the experimental design, while Tattle is building the tech platform to execute the study.

We're looking for participants for the study. So, if you or anyone you know would like to help build evidence around why Indians share bad or good information, please [sign up here](https://twitter.com/monkprayogshala/status/1511969587759394818).

We also started a [project with GitHub’s](https://socialimpact.github.com/insights/standardized-github-metrics-project-launch/) Social Impact and Policy teams for developing metrics from GitHub that can inform economics research and international development.

As an open source project building datasets for researchers, we understand first-hand the challenge of building and opening datasets responsibly; and evaluating the impact and benefits of open source projects.

Finally, Denny is putting his storytelling skills to work.

He has teamed up with [Adhiraj Singh](https://www.linkedin.com/in/ubima/) to develop an online role playing game that helps describe the negative externalities of sharing polarizing misinformation.

This game has received initial funding from the [Pluralism and Civic Exchange fund](https://www.discoursemagazine.com/ideas/2022/02/15/building-common-ground-in-a-divided-society/) at the Mercatus Center.

We don’t want to give too many spoilers but here’s the gist - by sharing inaccurate and polarizing information against their opponents players gain clout and go viral; but it also makes the game world spiral out of control.

If the first play tests are anything to go by, the game world is theater for some intense human drama, bringing out the best and the worst in people.
