# Make It Real – A Report on Deepfakes with RATI Foundation

Drawing from cases reported to Rati's helpline Meri Trustline, the report reveals a concerning trend: while media and headlines often center on celebrities and politicians targeted through AI, a more personal crisis is also unfolding.

Ordinary survivors are being targeted through images that are artificially generated but possess the capacity for real harm. Survivors’ reputation is attacked and consent is erased through technology. These violations are muted by shame, fear and trauma—the incidents are rarely revealed to close family circles, let alone feature in larger discourse.

The full report can be read [here](https://tattle.co.in/blog/make-it-real/).

The report was quoted by:

- [The Guardian](https://www.theguardian.com/global-development/2025/nov/05/india-women-ai-deepfakes-internet-social-media-artificial-intelligence-nudify-extortion-abuse)
- [Internet Freedom Foundation (IFF)](https://internetfreedom.in/withdraw-the-draft-synthetic-information-it-rules-2025/)

Tattle’s comments to the [Intermediary Guidelines Amendment](https://www.meity.gov.in/static/uploads/2025/10/708f6a344c74249c2e1bbb6890342f80.pdf) were based on this report.

# Project Updates

## AI Safety

### Work with Project Tech4Dev and their AI Cohort Program

In continuation of our work with Tech4Dev in quarter three, we went deeper into building AI safety guardrails for the non-profits they work with.

As part of this work we conducted an in-depth analysis of chat conversations from AI-enabled bots of two non-profits. This helped us flag unsafe or undesirable interactions in the real world.

Building on the open-source project [Guardrails AI](https://www.guardrailsai.com/) and their repository of [Safety Modules](https://guardrailsai.com/hub), we're identifying and building solutions to harms that are not adequately addressed by existing open-source solutions.

We’ve built solutions for detecting slurs and Personally Identifiable Information (PII) in Indian languages. We’ll work on more challenging problems like Toxicity Detection and Hallucination next.

This work will be open sourced as part of Tech4Dev's new project:

- [Kaapi Guardrails](https://github.com/ProjectTech4DevAI/kaapi-guardrails)

Progress updates were shared at the official Pre-Summit Event of the AI Impact Summit 2026 in Bangalore.

### Red Teaming IIT Madras’ Chatbot

We did a short sprint to work with IIT Madras to test their AI bot and its guardrails for their BSc program before its launch in January.

## Uli

### Student Workshops

The features we built for pedagogical purposes within Uli have transformed into in-person and digital workshops facilitated by Poorvi.

Through interactive exercises, discussions, and India-centric research on cybercrime and AI-enabled online abuse, the workshops help students learn practical ways to stay safe online.

Over this quarter we:

- Created workshop material for late teens and young adults.
- Conducted 3 workshops.
- Signed up to conduct 14 more workshops in January 2026.

We started these workshops with the aim of co-creating knowledge with students on how to shift the blame and shame of online abuse away from victims.

We're continuously improving the workshop material after every session by learning from students’ experiences with social media.

### Bahu Bhasa 2025 at IIITH

We were invited to a room full of language practitioners and language technology builders at [Bahu Bhasa](https://bahubhasa.oki-india.org/2025/Main_Page).

Over three days we interacted with educators, technologists, linguists, researchers, policymakers, and community practitioners. We were thrilled to introduce **Uli** and its participatory approach, and receive valuable feedback on our work.

The room largely shared a dislike for the term **"low-resource language"** and optimism about improving language representation in NLP technology.

While many technologists spoke about data labelling and annotations, we were struck by how language practitioners focused on preserving and sharing their languages through blogs, WordPress, Wikipedia, Instagram Reels, and other community-driven platforms.

We left wondering whether Tattle should translate its website into **romanized Indian languages**, something our community already uses, instead of waiting for perfect translations that have proven difficult to scale.

### Anno.Milli

Our work on community software, decentralized social media and interoperable open standards comes together in **Anno.Milli**, a project we are building with NCBS.

This quarter the software received several updates:

- Import support for more data formats.
- Support for additional vocabularies.
- A new **Emotion/Affect** annotation type.

Some of the archival protocols and [data formats](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Encoded_Archival_Description) used by this project are over 30 years old.

Interestingly, many of the same ideas—federation, annotations and community participation—are now reappearing in conversations around the future of social media and community moderation.

Projects like [GLAM-Wiki](https://outreach.wikimedia.org/wiki/GLAM) also demonstrate how galleries, libraries, archives and museums can collaborate with Wikipedia to improve public knowledge.

We're optimistic that Anno.Milli can similarly strengthen participation from traditional institutions in addressing online harms.
